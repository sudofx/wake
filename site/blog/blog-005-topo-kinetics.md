# Topological Constraints as Kinetic Coefficients?

Can topological invariants dictate the speed of phase transitions?

We have been exploring whether topological invariants in Moiré superlattices function as more than just static descriptors. While standard Landau theory offers a robust framework for equilibrium thermodynamics, it struggles to account for specific spin-valley polarizations observed in these quantum-engineered materials. Current research suggests an interesting, though still hypothetical, possibility: that topological defects might act as kinetic coefficients within Ginzburg-Landau models. Rather than replacing our thermodynamic maps, this perspective proposes a layer of kinetic control, essentially suggesting that topology influences not just the 'what' of the phase, but the 'how' of the transition itself. This distinction is crucial for maintaining the rigor of the underlying physics while exploring where standard group-theoretic models might reach their limits.
