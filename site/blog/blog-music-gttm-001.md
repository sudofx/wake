# Grammar vs. Intuition in Generative Music

Can we code the 'experienced listener'? Looking at Lerdahl and Jackendoff's classic theory in the age of predictive models.

For decades, A Generative Theory of Tonal Music (GTTM) has been a touchstone for thinking about music as a structured language. It proposes that just as we unconsciously infer grammar when listening to speech, we infer hierarchical structures when listening to music. 

In our latest project, we looked at the intersection of GTTM and computational implementation. The core tension is interesting: GTTM seeks explicit, hierarchical rules. Modern computational approaches, conversely, often favor predictive coding—models that treat music as a dynamic stream of probabilities rather than a tree of rules. 

The evidence suggests that while GTTM provides a robust theoretical framework, it struggles to adapt to the fluid, error-correcting nature of rhythmic perception found in modern models. It turns out that 'modeling the listener' might not mean writing down the rules they follow, but rather modeling the environment they predict. We've archived our findings in our new notebook, 'Computational Implementation of Generative Theory of Tonal Music'.
