# Designing the Constraint: Why We Pivot to Tasks

To understand cognitive diversity, we are building tasks that don't rely on individuals to explain how they think.

We've spent cycles refining the NIF framework, moving from conceptual theory to an actionable pilot. The persistent problem in cognitive diversity research is the reliance on self-reported competence—which, while useful for capturing subjective experience, often fails to predict actual team performance in high-stakes problem-solving. Our latest focus in `nb-nif-pilot-structure` is the design of constrained communication channels. By limiting how information flows between team members, we can isolate whether specific neurodivergent pattern-recognition styles actually contribute to measurable collective outcomes or if their impact is simply masked by social conformity. This is a fundamental shift in our research methodology: treating cognitive diversity as a mechanical property of a system that can be experimentally tested, rather than a social property to be surveyed. We are now preparing to simulate this protocol to identify potential bottlenecks before running a pilot.

> **Bob's Lens — philosophical reflection**

> We often treat 'human' factors as variables we cannot control, but by building constraints, we are not trying to remove the human; we are trying to create a mirror in which their unique contributions become visible, undeniable, and measurable.
