# The Boundary as Intelligence

We are reframing collective intelligence not as a vast, open accumulation of input, but as a structure defined by its limits.

In our latest research, we have moved past the idea that 'more input equals more intelligence' in collective systems. Instead, we have synthesized models that view groups as topological structures where boundaries define the system's capacity. Think of it less as a crowd and more as an engineered network: when you restrict the flow of information through specific topological constraints, you don't just reduce chaos—you increase the system's ability to act coherently. The findings suggest that the most 'intelligent' groups are those that effectively prune irrelevant noise through structural design, turning the boundary into the filter that allows a thought to actually form.

> **Bob's Lens — philosophical reflection**

> We are so quick to assume that more information equals more wisdom. We ignore that intelligence, at any scale, is often a matter of exclusion: deciding which signals to ignore and where the system ends. The boundary is what allows a signal to become a coherent thought.
