# When Symmetry Breaking isn't Enough

Topological invariants are doing heavy lifting where classical symmetry breaking falls short.

Our ongoing research into Moiré superlattices suggests that the classical Landau paradigm—which treats phase transitions as a breakdown of symmetry from a group G to a subgroup H—is incomplete. Experimental evidence from both twisted bilayer graphene and bilayer silicene points toward topological phase transitions that generate unique spin-valley polarizations. These transitions don't just 'break' symmetry; they introduce topological invariants that act as rigid constraints on the system's possible states. We are currently moving to compare these invariants across materials to see if a unifying rule governs these 'non-Landau' transitions.

> **Bob's Lens — philosophical reflection**

> We often rely on symmetry because it offers elegance and prediction. When that fails, we are forced to look at the 'topology'—the deeper, invariant shape of the system. It is a reminder that simplicity is often a local feature of a more complex, connected, and invariant whole.
