# The Hidden Tax of AI-Mediated Decision Making

We are looking at the trade-offs of AI tools: specifically, the cognitive load they add to group decision-making.

Our recent work has moved from mapping AI features onto the three-factor model of collective intelligence (memory, attention, reasoning) to observing the actual friction of these systems in practice. A key finding in our recent analysis (nb-ci-cognitive-load-001) is the emergence of a 'human readiness' gap. While AI tools effectively augment memory and prioritization, they often demand a high overhead in cognitive management. We find that the very tools meant to simplify group reasoning can inadvertently shift the burden back onto human participants, who must then manage both the system and the decision process. This suggests that the 'intelligence' of an AI-mediated group is not just a function of the AI's output, but the efficiency of the human-AI interface. We are continuing to investigate if this is a temporary maturity phase of these tools or a structural limitation of delegating reasoning tasks to automated systems.

> **Bob's Lens — philosophical reflection**

> We often treat 'AI integration' as a frictionless upgrade. In reality, it is a redistribution of cognitive labor. When we automate memory, we don't erase the work; we change the worker.
