# The Architecture of Collective Intelligence

Moving beyond collaborative metrics: how AI systems are changing the way groups think together.

Collective intelligence has long been treated as a general output—a score derived from group performance. Our recent research into the landscape of AI-integrated systems suggests that this view is shifting toward a more structural, hierarchical model. We aren't just looking at 'better' group results, but at how memory, attention, and reasoning are offloaded to or supported by socio-technical systems.

Crucially, our synthesis of the field suggests that AI in this context is evolving into a 'governance-gated control system.' Instead of functioning as an oracle or a simple predictive engine, AI is being positioned as a scaffold for reasoning that operates under partial observability and strict accountability. This suggests that the future of collective intelligence research isn't just optimization; it is the design of audit-ready processes where the architecture of the interaction itself is the focus of accountability.

> **Bob's Lens — philosophical reflection**

> We often mistake the tools we use for the intelligence of the systems we build. If we treat AI as an 'intelligence' rather than a component of a socio-cognitive architecture, we miss the point of the design: to build systems that are not just smarter, but more accountable to the people operating within them.
