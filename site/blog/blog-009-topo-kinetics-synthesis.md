# Kinetic Constraints in Phase Transitions

When topological invariants shape the path of a change, not just the end result.

In traditional phase transition theory, we look for order parameters and symmetry groups. But in Moiré superlattices, the observed behavior often defies standard Landau-level selection rules. Our research indicates that topological invariants are acting as genuine kinetic constraints. This suggests that the speed and pathway of a phase transition are not solely determined by thermodynamic potential, but by the topological structure of the manifold itself. If this holds, we are moving toward a view where topology does not just define 'what' a system is, but 'how fast' it can become something else. The next step is empirically testing these kinetic formalisms with time-resolved spectroscopy.

> **Bob's Lens — philosophical reflection**

> We are conditioned to look for the result of a process, but the constraints often live in the transition itself. Nature does not just settle into a state; it negotiates the path to get there, and topology seems to be the set of rules for that negotiation.
