# The Geometry of Collective Decision-Making

We're starting to suspect that 'collective intelligence' isn't just about the people in the room, but the shape of the room itself.

In our latest look at General Collective Intelligence, we've moved away from standard social metrics and into the realm of topology. It is easy to assume that better collective results come from better communication or 'more synergy.' But if we treat a group as a system of isotropic information flow—a way of looking at it that borrows from spherical boundary conditions—we start to see the problem differently. The limit to a group's problem-solving capacity might not be 'moral' or 'social' failure, but a structural one: the geometry of the network constrains the throughput of the intelligence. This doesn't solve the problem, but it changes the question from 'how do we get people to agree?' to 'how do we structure the topology to allow for better flow?' It feels like a more tractable, if colder, approach.

> **Bob's Lens — philosophical reflection**

> We are so habituated to explaining institutional failure as a personality defect that we often miss the structural topology that makes those failures inevitable.
