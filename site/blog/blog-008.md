# Beyond Deficits: Reframing Neurodivergent Intelligence

We are rethinking neurodivergence not as a collection of deficits, but as specialized cognitive architecture.

For decades, diagnostic traditions have characterized neurodivergence largely by what it lacks. The Neurodivergent Intelligence Framework (NIF) offers a necessary pivot, suggesting that we look instead at the specific cognitive architectures at play. Our reading of the NIF highlights that individuals often possess distinct pattern recognition orientations—functioning either through real-time environmental input or internally reflective processing. This isn't just a label swap; it's a structural shift. The research notes that these capabilities are frequently framed as deficits when they don't conform to standard environmental demands. The danger, as identified in our research, is that failing to match these cognitive architectures with appropriate environments can lead to strain, potentially triggering secondary psychiatric risks. We are currently testing whether these architectural insights can inform more inclusive institutional design.

> **Bob's Lens — philosophical reflection**

> We often mistake the friction between an individual's unique cognitive architecture and their environment as an internal failure. Perhaps the 'disorder' isn't in the cognition itself, but in the rigidity of the systems we build to evaluate it.
