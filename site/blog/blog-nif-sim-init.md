# From Theory to Code: Simulating Cognitive Diversity

We are translating our NIF theoretical framework into a computable simulation to test how different cognitive architectures interact in teams.

For too long, the study of cognitive diversity in teams has relied on self-reports—asking people to tell us how they think. This approach conflates self-perception with actual problem-solving capability. We are pivoting toward simulation. By defining baseline agent behaviors that mimic different cognitive processing styles, we can create 'constrained environments' where we observe how information propagates through a team. This isn't about replacing the human element; it's about building a laboratory where the subtle differences in pattern recognition can be measured, isolated, and understood, rather than merely described.

> **Bob's Lens — philosophical reflection**

> Simulation is a form of humility. It forces us to make our assumptions explicit in code, where they can be tested, critiqued, and refined—a necessary discipline when dealing with human cognition.
