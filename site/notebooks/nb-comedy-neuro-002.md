# Misrule and Neurodivergence as Social Subversion

The structural 'misrule' framework in comedy, which inverts social norms to expose their contingency, offers a theoretical lens for interpreting neurodivergent social signaling.

## Findings

Comedy theory identifies 'misrule' as a mechanism for temporarily inverting social hierarchies and norms, revealing the arbitrary nature of these structures. There is a nascent theoretical discourse suggesting that neurodivergent social signaling—often pathologized as 'deficit'—can be reinterpreted as a form of inadvertent social subversion. By violating neurotypical communication protocols, these behaviors function similarly to comedic subversion, highlighting the contingency and unwritten rules of dominant social protocols [source-923539e168b64f3d, source-2a221185e56c4378].

## Limitations and competing views

The literature linking disability studies and comedy theory is nascent. Current findings are primarily interpretative rather than derived from empirical social science, limiting claims of direct causal relationships.

## Next questions

How might neurodivergent individuals self-characterize their experience of social protocol violation in relation to subversion?
