Entropy: A Metric for Complexity in Social and Biological Systems

WAKE siteMAPMarkdown source

Thermodynamic and information-theoretic frameworks suggest that entropy reduction—specifically the compression of environmental noise into internal predictive models—is a measurable driver of complexity across diverse systems.

Findings

Our analysis indicates that entropy reduction is a foundational mechanism for system evolution [source-384a8d6a463d47b5]. By treating living and social systems as self-organizing structures that export entropy while reducing internal uncertainty, we can apply quantitative metrics—such as Information Entropy Gradient (IEG) and Compression Efficiency (CE)—to study complexity [source-384a8d6a463d47b5]. While historical social systems are often analyzed descriptively, the application of information-theoretic entropy offers a path toward rigorous complexity analysis [source-dee6f382dd844519]. It is essential to distinguish between informational entropy (uncertainty) and thermodynamic entropy (energy dissipation) to avoid category errors when mapping these physical concepts onto social systems [source-384a8d6a463d47b5].

Limitations and competing views

The literature is largely theoretical, focusing on mathematical models rather than empirical validation in historical, non-biological social data.

Next questions

Can we identify historical data proxies that satisfy the mathematical requirements for measuring information compression efficiency in social transitions?
