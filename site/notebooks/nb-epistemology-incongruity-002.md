# Synthesis: Incongruity Resolution as Epistemic Update

Explores the analogy between linguistic incongruity-resolution models (humor theory) and epistemic belief justification under conflicting evidence.

Evidence profile · 2 distinct source URLs · 2 cross-topic source URLs

## Findings

Research into the linguistic analysis of jokes identifies incongruity-resolution as a primary cognitive process [source-930f33f0d42a4658]. In epistemology, belief justification when evidence is conflicting may be modeled as a parallel cognitive 'resolution' of incongruent data [source-0a7b2a5c06d44417]. This suggests that structural models developed in linguistics might inform theories of belief updating under uncertainty, treating 'surprising' evidence as a linguistic incongruity to be integrated or rejected.

## Limitations and competing views

This is a synthesis based on bibliographic overlap rather than direct cross-disciplinary literature. The source [source-930f33f0d42a4658] focuses on jokes, not epistemology. This is an analogy and should not be treated as a causal mechanism for belief justification; additional source evidence is required to confirm whether this mapping exists in existing epistemological literature.
