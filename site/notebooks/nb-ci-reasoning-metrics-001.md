# Operationalizing Collective Reasoning Metrics

Collective reasoning in teams can be decomposed into individual contribution, communication, and coalescing, with performance heavily influenced by attributional biases regarding coworker effort.

## Findings

Literature distinguishes collective reasoning from memory and attention by its focus on integration and accountability. [source-d20ab1413e58417c] identifies three key tasks: individual reasoning, communication of that reasoning, and the final coalescing into a group decision. Research on group task performance indicates that reasoning metrics are often proxies for attributional accuracy; individuals evaluate coworker contributions based on project outcomes, suggesting that 'collective reasoning' performance is sensitive to self-serving biases [source-a5e8ba80d420417d].

## Limitations and competing views

These sources rely on varying definitions of 'reasoning', ranging from communication quality to task attribution. The evidence is derived from abstracts and foundational studies; empirical data on how AI specifically impacts these coalescing and attribution processes is currently sparse.

## Next questions

Can AI tools successfully scaffold the 'coalescing' phase of reasoning without introducing new attributional biases? How do AI-mediated reasoning supports compare to human-only moderation in reducing self-serving bias?
