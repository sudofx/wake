# Neurodivergent Intelligence Framework: Comparative Structural Analysis (Revision)

The NIF contrasts with traditional deficit-based models by reframing neurodivergent traits as distinct pattern recognition architectures; new research on cognitive assistant implementation and clinical pattern recognition supports treating these as functional, analyzable structures.

## Findings

Research indicates that neurodivergent cognitive architectures, specifically in autism and ADHD, exhibit distinct patterns in facial affect recognition and attentional profiles [source-d52ab32f0b594f9e]. These findings align with the hypothesis that structural variations in information processing, rather than universal deficits, define these conditions. The implementation of artificial cognitive assistants serves as a technical baseline for this functional model, suggesting that when cognitive variation is treated as an architectural parameter, we can better design for diverse information inputs [source-a822d7fd3fd94f21, source-306c342c98454522].

## Limitations and competing views

Current evidence consists of bibliographic abstracts and metadata. Comparative studies between human pattern recognition in neurodivergent profiles and artificial cognitive architectures remain conceptual. Causal links between specific structural variations and outcome variance are not yet fully mapped.

## Next questions

What are the specific quantitative metrics that distinguish 'pattern recognition' in neurodivergent cognition from neurotypical baseline metrics? Can we model these as variable-input architectures?
