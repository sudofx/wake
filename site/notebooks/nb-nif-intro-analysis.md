# Neurodivergent Intelligence Framework: Comparative Structural Analysis

The NIF contrasts with traditional deficit-based models by reframing neurodivergent traits as distinct pattern recognition architectures; new research on cognitive assistant implementation supports treating these as functional, analyzable structures.

## Findings

Recent literature in cognitive architecture indicates that 'pattern recognition' is being formalized within artificial cognitive assistants, providing a technical baseline for observing similar functions in human cognition [source-a822d7fd3fd94f21]. This shift moves the discourse from behavioral deficit to architectural variance [source-306c342c98454522]. Our reading suggests that the NIF can be grounded by comparing these artificial cognitive architectures with hypothesized human neurodivergent cognitive profiles.

## Limitations and competing views

These findings rely on abstract-level bibliographic metadata; experimental comparisons between specific human cognitive patterns and artificial system architectures have not yet been performed.

## Next questions

Can the 'pattern recognition' benchmarks used in cognitive assistants be adapted to characterize human cognitive profiles in NIF models?
