# Structural Functions of Misrule in Comedy

Misrule in Elizabethan comedy serves as a liminal space, allowing for the exploration of social and festive transgressions without necessarily undermining the established moral order, as these actions are bounded by the fictional frame of the theater.

## Findings

Turner (source-293e3b0a343f4e5a) identifies ritual and drama as mechanisms of public liminality where norms are suspended. In comedy, this 'misrule' acts as a structured release, providing a temporary suspension of social constraints [source-293e3b0a343f4e5a]. However, historical inquiry clarifies that in Elizabethan society, this dramatic 'misrule' was often a managed fiction, distinct from actual unchecked festive abuse, as officials enforced a clear boundary between theatrical representation and social practice [source-14fe1954198a4bcd].

## Limitations and competing views

The current scope focuses on Elizabethan drama and ritual theory; generalizability to modern satire or non-Western comedic structures remains an open question.

## Next questions

Does modern satirical comedy function as a 'safe' release valve in a similar liminal fashion, or do modern media environments dissolve the boundary between fictional 'misrule' and real-world social destabilization?
