# Functional vs. Integrative Socio-Cognitive Architectures for Human-Machine Intelligence

This notebook contrasts the tripartite functional model of collective memory, attention, and reasoning against the COHUMAIN integrative human-machine team framework.

## Findings

Based on the editorial 'AI for collective intelligence' [source-fc353e0b71524ce7], AI can be structured to enhance the socio-cognitive architecture of groups by targeting three specific operational pillars: collective memory, collective attention, and collective reasoning. In contrast, the COHUMAIN (Collective HUman-MAchine INtelligence) framework [source-968b3035b6384c08] focuses on the emergent capability of an integrated human-machine team to achieve goals across diverse environments. While the tripartite model [source-fc353e0b71524ce7] provides specific modular targets for cognitive enhancement, the COHUMAIN model [source-968b3035b6384c08] emphasizes the dynamics of integrated human-agent collaboration, identifying barriers to integration alongside philosophical and ethical concerns.

## Limitations and competing views

Both sources are editorial introductions or conceptual abstracts, lacking raw data or comparative empirical benchmarks in the provided texts. The specific mechanisms for mapping modular AI tools onto memory or reasoning remain high-level propositions.
