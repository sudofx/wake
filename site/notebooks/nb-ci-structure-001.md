# The Three-Factor Structure of Collective Intelligence

Collective intelligence can be modeled as a hierarchical system involving collective memory, collective attention, and collective reasoning, providing a framework to evaluate AI interventions.

## Findings

Research consistently identifies a general 'collective intelligence' (CI) factor, but structural modeling is converging on a three-factor hierarchy: memory, attention, and reasoning [source-1ba654396b7a4c16]. A meta-analysis of 22 studies and 1,356 groups demonstrates that this model explains variance in group performance better than alternatives, particularly in established groups [source-1ba654396b7a4c16]. Complementary editorial work posits that AI technologies may specifically support these three elements—enhancing the 'socio-cognitive architecture' of groups by scaffolding how they remember, attend to, and reason through problems [source-c23d5ed899ba4958].

## Limitations and competing views

The cited sources rely on meta-analytic correlations and editorial review rather than causal experimental data regarding specific AI-group interactions. The 'three-factor' model is a structural proposal that, while empirically supported in the cited dataset, remains a subject of ongoing theoretical refinement.

## Next questions

How do specific AI features (e.g., summarization, prioritization, debate-structuring) map onto these three factors? What are the measurable trade-offs when AI interventions shift the balance between collective memory and collective reasoning?
