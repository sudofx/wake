# Predictive Coding and Entropy in Non-Isochronous Rhythm

Information theory offers a quantifiable metric for modeling musical anticipation; predictive coding frameworks suggest listeners minimize surprise, with entropy acting as a proxy for prediction cost in non-isochronous structures.

## Findings

Information theory provides a robust framework for quantifying the statistical predictability of musical rhythms. Predictive coding models hypothesize that listeners actively model the statistical structure of auditory input to minimize prediction error. In the context of non-isochronous sequences, entropy measures can quantify the 'surprise' or 'cost' of predicting the next event, suggesting that rhythmic complexity may be an emergent property of these predictive states [source-54becf7641d14fb0, source-b889a58a4d2c4e35].

## Limitations and competing views

Most cited literature is abstract-level or derived from computational modeling. Experimental evidence directly bridging high-level cognitive musical anticipation with information-theoretic entropy in non-isochronous sequences remains sparse and requires further empirical validation.

## Next questions

Can human listener anticipation be empirically mapped to these entropy-based predictive models in real-time listening scenarios?
