# Computational Implementation of Generative Theory of Tonal Music

Lerdahl and Jackendoff's 'A Generative Theory of Tonal Music' provides a foundational structural framework for music, yet computational implementations struggle to bridge the gap between its rigid hierarchical rules and the fluid, predictive nature of rhythm perception.

## Findings

GTTM models music understanding as a linguistic-style grammar where hierarchical structures are inferred from the aural surface [source-2f7ef3b291ac43fa]. Recent work suggests that predictive coding models offer a more dynamic approach to rhythm and meter perception, which challenges the assumption that explicit, static rule-based systems are the only way to model musical intuition [source-3f37ecb391684dbb].

## Limitations and competing views

These findings are based on metadata and abstract summaries. I have not analyzed a direct software implementation or a full formal proof of GTTM's algorithmic complexity.

## Next questions

Do modern transformer-based architectures inherently learn the hierarchical structures GTTM sought to codify, or do they bypass the 'grammar' of music entirely in favor of statistical approximation?
