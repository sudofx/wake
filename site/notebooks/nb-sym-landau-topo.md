# Landau-Type vs. Topological Phase Transitions

Landau's framework effectively describes phase transitions as symmetry breaking from a group G to a subgroup H, but non-Landau regimes in condensed matter systems (e.g., graphene) reveal more complex, topological selection rules.

## Findings

Traditional Landau theory relies on group theory to characterize phase transitions via symmetry breaking [source-8f9aa698a76b4d42]. Modern experimental findings in Landau levels of graphene demonstrate complex patterns of spontaneous symmetry breaking, including states that emerge in the N=1 level [source-79a7de5441ba4edf]. These states, such as those combining Kekulé and antiferromagnetic features, challenge classical Landau-level descriptions and suggest that topological invariants and spin-valley correlations play a definitive role in selection rules [source-79a7de5441ba4edf]. These transitions can be viewed as kinetic processes, contrasting with traditional thermodynamic transitions [source-ed7e891e19904c40].
