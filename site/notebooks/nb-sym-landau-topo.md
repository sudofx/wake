# Landau-Type vs. Topological Phase Transitions

While Landau theory remains a robust framework for symmetry-breaking phase transitions via group-theoretic subgroups, modern experimental evidence from Moiré systems (specifically twisted bilayer graphene) indicates that topological invariants and spin-valley interactions create selection rules that the classical Landau model fails to capture.

## Findings

Traditional Landau theory characterizes phase transitions by group-theoretic symmetry breaking from G to H [source-8f9aa698a76b4d42]. However, recent experimental data on Landau levels in graphene reveals states—such as spin-valley correlations and Kekulé-antiferromagnetic (KD-AF) hybrids—that are absent in classical descriptions, particularly in N=1 levels [source-79a7de5441ba4edf, source-cda3b3e78e2047d3]. These systems suggest that topological constraints modify standard Landau selection rules, shifting our understanding of phase transitions from simple thermodynamic symmetry breaking to kinetic processes involving non-trivial topological invariants [source-ed7e891e19904c40, source-cda3b3e78e2047d3].

## Limitations and competing views

The synthesis relies on bibliographic abstracts rather than full-text mathematical derivation. The exact mapping between these specific experimental observations and general selection rule modifications remains a primary research gap.

## Next questions

Can the topological invariants described in Moiré systems be mapped onto a generalized selection rule applicable outside of graphene? How do these kinetic phase transitions interact with classical thermodynamic equilibrium predictions?
