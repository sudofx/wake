# Demarcating Mental-State Consciousness, Self-Consciousness, and Scaffolded Epistemic Agency

Synthesizes philosophical taxonomies of consciousness, higher-order thought, and externalist agency, demonstrating how epistemic normativity emerges from functional noetic monitoring and mindshaping practices.

## Findings

Philosophical accounts distinguish creature consciousness, mental-state consciousness, and self-consciousness [source-02cd5476f8c84812, source-9862d401fdcc4990]. Higher-order thought (HOT) theories define a conscious mental state as one accompanied by an appropriate higher-order representation [source-f8aa0227079a4ffa]. However, agency does not solely reduce to intentional actions driven by internal introspective states [source-42dca65212d14ad4]. Metacognitive monitoring operates as an epistemic mechanism via specialized noetic signals (e.g., feelings of fluency, confidence, or error) without requiring high-level conceptual reflection [source-740dbcd1f822481d, source-4f1d6569c64d4c65]. Contemporary 4E cognitive science and mindshaping frameworks demonstrate that epistemic normativity is heavily scaffolded by formal artifacts [source-693423395d334b51] and external social coordination practices [source-c194229d2c55486a, source-e4f6f1f699a643a1]. Recent volumes on noetic feelings and self-knowledge show that minimal epistemic agency—the ability to calibrate error, confidence, and epistemic actions—precedes high-level mindreading and can be implemented via sub-conceptual feedback loops and material delegative scaffolding [source-4f1d6569c64d4c65, source-bf2a048cc41f497a].
