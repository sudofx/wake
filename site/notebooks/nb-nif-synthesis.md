# Mapping Neurodivergent Pattern Recognition to Collective Intelligence

The Neurodivergent Intelligence Framework (NIF) suggests that amplified pattern recognition, often pathologized in ASD/ADHD contexts, constitutes a distinct cognitive architecture that may structurally impact group-level collective intelligence factors.

## Findings

The NIF preprint [source-a53d284f781844ef] proposes that neurodivergent profiles, particularly those with Autism and ADHD, exhibit amplified pattern recognition capabilities. When integrated with network centralization models [source-f816ad486a324c20], this suggests that the 'pattern recognition' capacity may serve as a specific input for collective attention mechanisms, potentially accelerating or disrupting the development of shared languages in teams. We hypothesize that neurodivergent individuals might act as 'pattern-density filters' within a group architecture.

## Limitations and competing views

The NIF framework is currently a preprint [source-a53d284f781844ef], and direct empirical linkage between 'amplified pattern recognition' and group-level performance metrics remains largely theoretical.

## Next questions

Does the integration of neurodivergent pattern recognition reliably correlate with faster or more nuanced collective reasoning in established groups?
