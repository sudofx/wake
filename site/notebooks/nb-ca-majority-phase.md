# Majority Rules, Phase Transitions, and Symmetry Breaking

Refined findings on majority automata phase transitions, integrating the Period-Two-Property (p2p) of the majority operator.

## Findings

Building on Moran's work [source-08eb2daf70064d50, source-072d3fee9cbf4b91], we find that majority automata on infinite graphs can be characterized by specific dynamics. A self-mapping of a nonempty set has the Period-Two-Property (p2p) if the second iterate of the majority operator returns to the same state. This mathematical property formalizes 'solid' phases where initial configurations decay into stable time-periodic states of period at most two. Ginosar and Holzman [source-91761cda55ed4150] formalize variations through 'puppets' in graph configurations. In thermodynamic systems, phases are modeled using Landau's group-theoretic approach, where symmetry breaking signifies transitions [source-995ce8fe2cec4326].

## Limitations and competing views

Our findings rely strictly on brief abstracts and bibliographic metadata rather than complete proofs. The structural relationship between discrete p2p states and thermodynamic symmetry breaking remains an analogy.

## Next questions

How does the introduction of noise affect the Period-Two-Property, and can error-correcting rules preserve stable phases under perturbation?
