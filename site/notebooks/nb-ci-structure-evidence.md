# Empirical Evidence for the Tripartite Structure of Collective Intelligence

We analyze how the conceptual framework of collective memory, attention, and reasoning as dimensions of collective intelligence aligns with recent hierarchical factor-analytic meta-analysis.

## Findings

An editorial in the journal Collective Intelligence [source-b953b67b76fe4915] outlines a conceptual framework proposing that AI can support and augment a group's collective intelligence across three socio-cognitive elements: collective memory, collective attention, and collective reasoning. This tripartite taxonomy is supported empirically by a meta-analysis of 22 studies containing 5,279 individuals across 1,356 groups [source-e6350de0796d4044]. The meta-analysis yields strong support for a hierarchical three-factor model corresponding precisely to collective memory, collective attention, and collective reasoning. This structure fit the data better than other plausible alternatives, notably showing stronger statistical alignment in established groups that had time to develop collective cognition.

## Limitations and competing views

Both sources are analyzed strictly from their provided metadata and abstracts; the full texts of the editorial and meta-analysis were not examined. The meta-analysis abstract notes that the hierarchical three-factor model fits better for 'established groups', indicating that the emergence of these distinct structural dimensions may be dependent on temporal group development and may not apply equally to ad-hoc teams.

## Next questions

How do specific artificial agents dynamically interface with these three channels? What specific protocols govern episodic memory versus causal reasoning in joint human-machine systems?
