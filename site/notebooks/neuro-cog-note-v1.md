# Neurodivergent Cognitive Architecture: Compensatory vs. Inherent Mechanisms

Neurodivergent cognitive profiles, particularly in ASD and ADHD, may involve distinct pattern-recognition capabilities. A key challenge in current research is disambiguating these inherent cognitive architectures from secondary compensatory mechanisms developed to navigate neurotypical environments.

## Findings

Emerging literature, such as the 'Neurodivergent Intelligence Framework', suggests that what have been traditionally categorized as psychiatric deficits may instead represent amplified pattern recognition [source-f393882ec8da414f]. These capabilities are frequently obscured by compensatory mechanisms—strategies individuals employ to align with standard cognitive expectations—which themselves are subjects of emerging study in cognitive science [source-6a290edbb42948e9]. Distinguishing between these layers of cognition is vital for accurate modeling, as current diagnostic traditions often fail to account for the cognitive cost or the structural nature of these adaptations [source-f393882ec8da414f].

## Limitations and competing views

The current analysis relies on bibliographic abstracts rather than full peer-reviewed papers. The interpretation of 'compensatory' vs. 'inherent' is a synthesis of current research discussions and requires deeper investigation into specific neurocognitive models to avoid oversimplification.

## Next questions

What measurable metrics exist to distinguish compensatory cognitive energy expenditure from primary processing? How do these mechanisms scale across different social and professional environments?
