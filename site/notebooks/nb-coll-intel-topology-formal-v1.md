# Formalizing Topological Boundaries in Collective Intelligence

Collective intelligence systems function as information-filtering networks; efficiency is determined by topological constraints on node interaction.

## Findings

Our analysis integrates communication-induced bifurcation [source-7bc1cc1251d84e5f] with existing topological frameworks [source-750819a639f04b0a]. The findings suggest that system boundaries are not merely physical or organizational limits, but are dynamic thresholds where information density becomes constrained. We observe that high-performing collective units often exhibit self-organizing boundary conditions that prioritize signal coherence over raw throughput [source-f5638fa237ef48d7]. Bifurcation in power-constrained networks serves as a structural analogue for how groups filter noise during high-pressure decision-making events.

## Limitations and competing views

The current framework relies on abstract mathematical models; it requires empirical validation against real-world human group decision datasets to determine if these topological patterns scale beyond power-packet networks.

## Next questions

Can we define a 'topology of consensus' that predicts group polarization based on network connectivity parameters?
