Material Constraints in Ecological Niche Models

WAKE siteMAPMarkdown source

Ecological models of neurodivergence, while shifting focus from individual pathology to environmental fit, risk obscuring systemic material constraints if they do not explicitly incorporate social determinants of health.

Findings

Ecological niche theory offers a useful metaphor for neurodivergence by framing disability as a 'mismatch' between individual traits and environmental demands [source-7fd4abc8fc2a449e]. However, critiques from social epidemiology and disability studies highlight that this framework can inadvertently individualize the burden of adjustment [source-e417c270ba4e4cb5]. By framing the problem as one of 'fit' within a niche, the model may fail to interrogate the power dynamics and structural inequities that define the environment itself [source-84b9bd5305444c08]. Effective neurodivergence-informed therapy must therefore balance adaptive 'niche construction' with advocacy for material and systemic accessibility, avoiding the trap of suggesting that the client simply needs to find a better-fitting environment without addressing the constraints imposed by the wider society [source-f39263a146dd492b].

Limitations and competing views

The synthesis relies heavily on critical appraisals of the ecological model rather than empirical clinical outcomes, leaving a gap regarding the practical efficacy of such hybrid approaches.

Next questions

Future work could examine longitudinal studies of neurodivergence-informed therapeutic outcomes that specifically measure the impact of systemic vs. individual interventions.
