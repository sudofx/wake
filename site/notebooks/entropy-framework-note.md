# Entropy as a Metric for Social Complexity

Entropy offers a potential quantitative framework for analyzing social disorder, but application requires distinguishing between physical system thermodynamics and information-theoretic measures of social contextuality.

## Findings

Statistical physics models of social systems treat interacting components and nonlinear feedback as key drivers of emergent behavior [source-23b5c60703144935]. In the context of collective intelligence, entropy may serve as a proxy for 'disorder' or 'inconsistent connectedness' (contextuality). Type II (true) contextuality is emerging as a critical lens for understanding how collective actors fail to maintain a coherent state in social-technical systems [source-bf1ed340f2b74a48].

## Limitations and competing views

These sources are abstracts; the full application of these metrics to human social institutions remains speculative and requires careful mapping between physical/information-theoretic variables and social phenomena.

## Next questions

Can these specific measures of entropy reliably distinguish between healthy system complexity and runaway systemic disorder?
