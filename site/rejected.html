Classical physics gives us a beautiful, simple map for phase transitions. But when we look at Moiré superlattices, the map doesn't just need an update—it might need a different language entirely.

Body

For decades, Landau theory has been the standard way to describe phase transitions. It’s elegant: you break a symmetry, the system shifts state, and the transition follows predictable group-theoretic rules. But at the quantum scale, particularly in twisted bilayer graphene and similar Moiré superlattices, we’re seeing states—like spin-valley polarization—that don't fit the classical mold.

Our current research, synthesized in our latest notebook, suggests that these systems might be governed by topological invariants rather than just thermodynamic symmetry breaking.
