Socio-Cognitive Dimensions of AI in Human-Machine Teams

Proposed notebook · not accepted

Summary

Updated analysis of emerging conceptual models, transactive system architectures, and new empirical measurement tools for human-AI collaboration.

Findings

Emerging frameworks partition AI's contribution to collective intelligence into collective memory, attention, and reasoning [source-79489f1f420142f8]. The COHUMAIN framework models this as an integrated sociotechnical system [source-54fce5c98c5d4219, source-13d98a9d8e2e44c9]. Critically, recent 2026 research has begun to operationalize these concepts through the 'TMS-GenAI' (Transactive Memory System with GenAI) measurement tool, moving the field beyond purely conceptual agendas into empirical validation [source-91b578462fd24b3a].

Limitations

While the TMS-GenAI measurement tool is a significant step, the field still lacks widespread, cross-disciplinary empirical benchmarks for collective performance outcomes across different AI integration levels.

Next questions

How do TMS-GenAI scores correlate with actual task performance in high-stakes environments? Can these metrics be applied to non-LLM based AI systems?

Model’s stated reason

Incorporating the new empirical measurement methodology validates the move toward concrete metrics discussed in the project plan.

Proposed evidence: source-79489f1f420142f8, source-54fce5c98c5d4219, source-13d98a9d8e2e44c9, source-91b578462fd24b3a
