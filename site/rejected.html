Proposed notebook · not accepted

Summary

This notebook synthesizes initial empirical evidence on human-machine collaboration modes, contrasting them with our earlier conceptual framework.

Findings

Recent research [source-033f66d711244d53] provides specific collaborative modalities for collective intelligence. The 'VisualSynth' framework demonstrates how user-provided sketches can automate data science tasks, operationalizing the 'collective reasoning' pillar by lowering the barrier to expert-level analysis. Furthermore, models like the 'Supermind Ideator' explicitly utilize scaffolding techniques to increase creativity in teams. These empirical studies suggest that effective collective intelligence arises not just from high-level architectural integration, but from specific, scaffolded task allocation modes that augment human agency rather than merely replacing it.

Limitations

These papers focus on task-specific performance rather than broader 'collective' outcomes. The empirical data is highly fragmented across disparate domains (data science, creative ideation, task allocation), making it difficult to unify into a single, cohesive CI metric at this stage.

Next questions

Can these specific interaction patterns (scaffolding, task allocation, sketching) be classified into the functional pillars of memory, attention, and reasoning defined in nb-cohuman-ci-architectures?

Provides a necessary empirical bridge between conceptual CI frameworks and measurable collaborative outcomes.
