# Evaluate independent reasoning

Status: proposed protocol; no recruited participants, collected responses or
demonstrated educational benefit. The ant-farm comparison does not collect study
data. Technical tests establish instrument behavior, not changes in people.

## Question and outcome

Does making a prediction and inspecting a controlled simulation comparison help
people evaluate a different explanation without continued WAKE guidance?

Primary outcome: independently scored performance on an unseen transfer task.
Completion clicks, visit length, enthusiasm and return visits are not outcomes of
independent reasoning. Secondary outcomes are observation/interpretation
distinction, identifying disconfirming evidence, and calibrated uncertainty.

## Before recruiting

Use deterministic fixtures to verify question presentation and scoring first.
Then prespecify the study design, task versions, allocation, primary comparison,
sample-size justification, scoring rubric, exclusions, missing-response handling,
analysis and stopping rule. Freeze these before reviewing outcomes. Keep pilot
task refinement separate from a later confirmatory study; don't reuse exposed
tasks as unseen tests. This document supplies a protocol outline, not a justified
sample size or statistical power claim.

Any initial human pilot must be separately authorized and use consenting adults.
Explain participation, optional withdrawal, collected information and retention
before the activity. Do not recruit the user's students or vulnerable groups by
default. Any future work involving minors or vulnerable participants requires
appropriate safeguards, consent arrangements and review before recruitment.
No deployment silently begins a study or adds behavioral collection.

## A small controlled design

1. Give participants a baseline reasoning task unrelated to ants, such as deciding
   what evidence could distinguish two explanations for a plant growing unevenly.
2. Allocate participants randomly to the prediction/test/evidence experience or a
   matched observation-only farm explanation. Keep exposure opportunity and
   instructions comparable; do not require a particular time on site.
3. Present a distinct, previously unseen transfer task, such as investigating why
   one bicycle tire loses pressure faster. Provide enough information to reason
   without specialist knowledge. Do not let the page guide the answer.
4. Ask what was observed, what is inferred, two plausible explanations, one test
   that could distinguish them, and what could reasonably be concluded.
5. Have independent scorers, unaware of condition, apply the frozen rubric.
   Check scorer agreement and resolve disagreements through the declared process.
6. End the activity clearly. No return visit, account, proof of offline activity
   or continued engagement is required.

Use different equivalent baseline/transfer versions and balance their ordering
to reduce task-specific familiarity. Test that examples are understandable and
accessible before freezing the study. Baseline imbalance, missing responses and
alternative explanations remain visible in reporting.

## Scoring rubric

Score each dimension 0 (absent/incorrect), 1 (partly correct), or 2 (clear/correct):

| Dimension | Evidence for full credit |
| --- | --- |
| Observation | Separates supplied observations from explanations. |
| Alternatives | Gives two distinct explanations compatible with those observations. |
| Falsifiability | Names an observation that would count against a stated explanation. |
| Test design | Changes or compares one relevant condition while accounting for confounds. |
| Uncertainty | Limits the conclusion to the evidence and leaves unresolved alternatives open. |

The transfer score ranges from 0 to 10. Do not award credit for agreeing with
WAKE or using particular terminology. Publish example scored fixture answers,
including an uncertain but well-reasoned response and a confident unsupported
one, before using the rubric for a study. The rubric is itself unvalidated until
piloted; report that limitation.

## Privacy and interpretation

Collect only explicitly consented task responses and assigned condition, under a
separately approved retention plan. Avoid names, accounts, device fingerprints,
interaction recordings and default analytics. Keep recruitment/consent handling
separate from the public experiment. Publish only approved, non-identifying
aggregates and methods; a small cohort can remain identifying despite removing
names.

Report effect sizes and uncertainty for the prespecified comparison. Include
negative, inconclusive and unavailable results, missingness and scoring limits.
A small immediate transfer effect would not establish lasting independence,
reduced dependency, benefits for young people, or broader life outcomes. Those
require separate studies. An inconclusive result is a legitimate outcome and
must not trigger unplanned changes to the primary metric.
