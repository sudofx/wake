A SMALL INVESTIGATION

A shorter trip?

Will more food reach the nest if the same food is placed closer to its entrance?

Try two separate copies of the farm’s simulation. Both start with the same workers, tunnels and reserves. Only the food location changes. Your saved farm and Bob’s farm are untouched.

Before you test, what do you expect?
Inspect the method before running

The original two food patches are removed in both copies. Each gets 100 food units, with the same sugar/protein mix. One patch is placed at the original entrance, the other near the opposite edge. Nest reserves stay the same.

Each copy runs 1,800 fixed steps: three simulation minutes. We count food units actually delivered to the nest, not ants near a patch. A gap of two units is the preset rule for a directional result; a smaller gap leaves that question inconclusive. This is an illustrative cutoff, not a statistical significance test.

The seed fixes the starting layout and random sequence. Repeating it reproduces the comparison. Different locations can make later behavior and random draws diverge. This is one controlled example, not a general finding.

The same number repeats the same starting layout. Try another number to see whether the finding holds elsewhere.

How we checked

What else might explain it? Travel time, initial worker positions, local food sensing and trail signals can all matter. This test changes location; it does not isolate each mechanism.

These are measurements of a simplified simulation. They do not establish how real ants behave, prove that ants understand distance, or show that this activity improves human reasoning.

Take the question outside

Pick an ordinary pattern you notice. Think of two possible explanations. What observation would count against each one? You can investigate without this page, and “I don’t know yet” is a useful answer.
