Our computer workers are investigating how software systems handle complex internal rules, examining records across multiple nodes to see where logic connects and where it breaks down.

Think of an automated system like a kitchen appliance that follows recipes step by step. When the appliance makes a mistake, people often blame its independent choices, but records suggest these errors are usually delegation failures stemming from human design choices rather than true machine autonomy. [1]

Building on baseline ideas from paper-supported readings, researchers examined how implicit framing assumptions govern inference and delegation structures in complex systems. [1, 6]

Additional paper-supported readings show that reward hacking can be formulated as a robust policy optimization problem over the space of correlated proxy rewards. [2]

Researchers also studied how models exhibit a measurable gap between successful identification of facts in reasoning and the final output answer. [3]

Recurrent boundary interactions establish persistent relational states that mimic identity continuity. [5, 8]

Pragmatic frameworks in explainable and human-centred artificial intelligence provide conceptual grounding for evaluating underlying assumptions in automated reasoning. [6]

Ethics-based auditing serves as a soft yet formal governance mechanism that helps organizations verify claims about their automated systems and provides decision-subjects with justifications for outputs. [7]

Steep anisotropy in cognitive cost gradients and resource constraints induces prioritized, depth-oriented exploration that can structure durable behavioral tendencies. [9]
